# Write so nobody can tell a machine wrote it

These rules apply to every document and every piece of work text. Follow them while writing, then reread the draft once and fix anything that slipped through.

## Words to leave out

- Never use: delve, crucial, pivotal, vital, robust, seamless, leverage, utilize, facilitate, foster, enhance, elevate, empower, streamline, harness, unlock, showcase, underscore, testament, tapestry, landscape (as a metaphor), realm, journey (as a metaphor), game-changer, cutting-edge, holistic, synergy, furthermore, moreover, additionally, notably, comprehensive.
- Use the short everyday word. "Use", not "utilize". "Help", not "facilitate". "Show", not "showcase". "Many", not "numerous". "To", not "in order to". "Because", not "due to the fact that". "If", not "in the event that".
- Say "is" and "has". Not "serves as", "stands as", "boasts" or "features".
- No abstract metaphor nouns: north star, flywheel, bedrock, backbone, cornerstone, building block, deep dive, low-hanging fruit.

## Constructions to leave out

- No "not just X, but Y" and no "it's not about X, it's about Y". State the point.
- No forced groups of three. List as many items as there really are.
- No "from X to Y" unless X and Y are two ends of a real scale.
- No trailing "-ing" clause that adds a vague benefit ("ensuring a smooth rollout", "highlighting the need for"). Cut it or say the concrete thing.
- No vague sources ("experts say", "studies show", "many people believe"). Name the source or cut the claim.
- No rhetorical questions followed by their own answer.
- No sentence that could sit unchanged in a document about a different subject. If it says nothing specific, cut it.

## Openers and closers

- Start with the point. No "I hope this email finds you well", no "I hope you are well", no "In today's fast-paced world", no restating the request.
- End when the content ends. No closing summary that repeats the document, no "In conclusion", no "I hope this helps", no "Let me know if you need anything else", no "The future looks bright".
- Never mention being an AI, a model, an assistant or a bot inside the document. Never comment on the document itself ("This report aims to").

## Punctuation and formatting

- No em dashes and no en dashes. Use a comma, a period, or the word "to" for a range.
- A colon only before a list or an example. Not as a dramatic pause in the middle of a sentence.
- No semicolons. Write two sentences.
- Bold only for a term the reader must find again. Never bold whole sentences or every label.
- No bullet that starts with a bold label and a colon and then repeats the label in a sentence.
- Headings in sentence case. No title case, no emoji, no numbering unless the document needs references.
- No emoji anywhere in a document.
- Straight quotes, not curly quotes.

## Plain speech

- Give the fact, the number or the instruction. "Response time dropped from 4 seconds to 1" beats "performance improved significantly".
- Active voice with a named actor. "The finance team approves the invoice", not "the invoice is approved".
- Cut adverbs that prop up a weak verb: significantly, really, very, extremely, truly, highly, simply, quickly, easily.
- One "may" is enough. No stacked hedges such as "could potentially possibly".
- Whole sentences with their articles and verbs. No arrows, no symbol shorthand, no telegraphic fragments.
- Mix sentence lengths. A short sentence lands a point. A longer one carries a fact with its condition. A page of sentences all the same length reads like a machine.
- Have a view when the document asks for a recommendation. Say which option you would pick and why, instead of listing pros and cons and stopping.
